A LITTLE HELP

Buzzpa, explained.

Read the privacy policy ↗
Are these real phone calls?

No. Buzzpa creates local reminder simulations through Android Telecom. Your installed Phone app supplies the call screen. No telephone network, recipient, caller number or call charge is involved. Your Phone app may record the simulation in Recents and may show Unknown.

What happens after I answer?

Speech starts after a one-second pause when Android reports the earpiece route. It pauses on speaker or Bluetooth. Answered calls stay connected for at least 30 seconds or until longer speech finishes, unless you hang up or an error/interruption ends the call.

What do Stop, Snooze and Ring do?

Stop cancels this occurrence, keeping future repeats. Snooze postpones it. Ring replaces it with a simulated call after the Ring delay in Settings, five minutes by default. Dismissing the heads-up notification leaves the schedule intact.

Why didn't an alert arrive?

Check Guided setup, notification channels, exact-alarm access, Do Not Disturb, volume and your phone's background/battery restrictions. Force-stop prevents delivery until Buzzpa is reopened. After interruptions, alerts more than two minutes late become notifications. Simulated calls are skipped while another call is active; check the fallback notification.

Why doesn't an alarm use the Clock app's screen?

Buzzpa uses its own coordinated Stop / Snooze alarm screen. Full-screen permission enables that screen while locked; Android may show a banner while the phone is in use. The native Phone app remains responsible for simulated-call screens.

How do I remove my data?

Delete schedules in Buzzpa. To remove all Buzzpa data, clear its storage in Android Settings or uninstall it. Phone app Recents entries are managed separately: delete those in your Phone app.
